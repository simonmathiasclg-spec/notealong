## Notes
* None skipped: every author's death year was checked (Wikipedia; Gerard via the archived Morrison Foundation page) and all died 1942 or earlier except Von Tilzer 1956, Norworth 1959, Armstrong 1951, Sterling 1955, Madden 1952 (all before 1972).
* Melody sources: original scans read for Daisy Bell (1892), Bill Bailey (1902), Give My Regards to Broadway (1904, Internet Archive), Shine On Harvest Moon (1908), Grand Old Flag (1906 interim edition); the rest from the typeset Public Domain Song Anthology (Open Book Publishers, https://doi.org/10.32881/book2, on Commons), cross-checked with memory and ABC where available. IMSLP PDFs are behind a captcha and were not downloaded.
* Uncertain points: Grand Old Flag wording/"boast or brag" contour (see row); Broadway verse bar "pier" has two extra eighths that only verse 2 uses, written as a melisma on "pier"; Bill Bailey dialect respelled in standard English; Sweet Adeline opening "Sweet Ad-e-" (D5 C5 F4) taken from the anthology only.

## Notes and uncertainties
* All 10 written files pass `build --check` (`ok`, no FAIL). The remaining warnings are notes outside the key (chromatic notes that are really in the melody) and "chords will repeat" where the refrain is sung twice.
* Melodies were read by eye from scans of the published sheet music (Internet Archive) for Moonlight Bay, Over There, Pack Up Your Troubles, Down by the Old Mill Stream, Oh You Beautiful Doll, You Made Me Love You, Peg o' My Heart, Let Me Call You Sweetheart (chorus) and Tipperary (chorus). I Want a Girl follows Colin Hume's ABC, which cites the Levy sheet music; it was not checked against the scan.
* You Made Me Love You: in "You made me want you" / "I want some love that's true", the second note was read as D5 (it could be C5). The first bar was read as E4 B4 A4 G4. The scan is a later Broadway Music Corp. reprint, which keeps the 1913 song.
* Oh, You Beautiful Doll: "I want to hug you but I fear you'd break" was read as B, A#, B, A#, G, A# (the sharp carries through the bar). "without you" ends on Fb4, as printed.
* Down by the Old Mill Stream: the chorus pickup "Down by the" is printed as eighth notes under fermatas. It is written here as three quarter notes, which is how the first-ending return is printed. The source scan is a later Canadian edition (Old Mill restaurant).
* Let Me Call You Sweetheart: written in C (the original is in Bb). The chromatic run after "love me too" is in the piano part, not the voice.
* Chords are simplified from the piano parts. Where the scan's harmony was unclear, ordinary Tin Pan Alley harmony was used.
